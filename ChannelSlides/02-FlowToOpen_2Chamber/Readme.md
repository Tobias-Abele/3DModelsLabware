# FlowToOpen_2Chamber Slide



Create **efficient flow chambers** or **large open reservoirs** with minimal liquid volumes!  
The **FlowToOpen_2Chamber** slide is designed with two curved chambers and side spacers, making it ideal for flexible lab experiments — from cell culture to solution flow studies.  
✨ *Bonus*: Built to fit standard **ibidi lids**!

---

## 🔧 General Use

This slide was created for easy lab workflows:

- Two chambers with elegant curved cutouts allow smooth liquid handling.
- Side spacers are designed for placing an **18 mm × 18 mm glass coverslip** on top.
- Built to fit **ibidi lids** for seamless integration.
- Simple design makes it easy to switch between a sealed flow chamber and an open reservoir mode.
- Designed to be glued onto a **standard glass slide** (23.5 mm × 50 mm size) for stability and reusability.

## ⚡ Quick Start

You can get started in two ways:

### ➤ **Option 1: Use the Pre-made STL File**

1. Download the `.stl` file.
3. Import the STL into your 3D printer software and print (recommended: PLA on textured PEI plate).

### ➤ **Option 2: Customize in Blender**

1. Download and install [Blender 4.3.2](https://www.blender.org/download/).
2. Download and open the `FlowToOpen_2Chamber.blend` file.
3. Adjust dimensions or features if desired.
4. Export your customized version as an STL and print:  
   - Go to **File → Export → STL (.stl)**  
   - Make sure to tick:
     - ✅ **Selection Only**
     - ✅ **Apply Modifiers**

## 🔬 Laboratory Use Tips

This slide is *tested and optimized* for flow experiments and minimal-volume cell work!

### 🖨️ Printing & Preparation
- Print on a **textured PEI plate** for best adhesion and a slightly rough surface that helps with glue bonding.
- For best results, use **PVA filament as support material** during printing, especially for clean cutouts.  
  (Recommended: water-soluble PVA like *PolyDissolve* — simply dissolve it in water after printing!)
- After printing, use a **silicon-based two-component glue** (like dentist silicone glue) to bond the slide onto a glass slide.

---

## 🧫 Assembling a Flow Chamber
1. **First, glue the printed slide onto a standard glass slide** (dimensions: **23.5 mm × 50 mm**) using the two-component silicone glue. Let it dry completely.
2. Place an **18 mm × 18 mm glass coverslip** on top of the side spacers (this forms the ceiling of your flow chamber).
3. Gently fill liquid into one chamber — it will flow into the second chamber through the middle gap.
4. Make sure liquid is present underneath the top glass coverslip and inside the chamber.
5. Seal the top edges with the two-component glue (or fit an ibidi lid!) and let it dry.  
   ✅ Now your flow chamber is ready — ideal for flushing in cells or solutions with minimal volume!

---

## 🔄 Pro Tip: Convert Back to Open Reservoir!

- After your experiment, you can *peel off* the top glue, remove the glass coverslip, and instantly convert the sealed flow chamber back into a large open reservoir.  
  ➡️ *Perfect* for adding larger volumes of cell medium or easy access for pipetting!

---

## 💧 Direct Use as Open Chamber

- You can also **skip adding the top coverslip** from the start and directly use the slide as a dual-chamber reservoir.
- The curved chambers make pipetting safe and smooth — ideal for avoiding scratching delicate cell layers.
